# Respondeo (excerpt)

I answer that, As shown above (I:87:2), habits are known by their acts. Now the act of hope is a movement of the appetitive faculty, since its object is a good. And, since there is a twofold appetite in man, namely, the sensitive which is divided into irascible and concupiscible, and the intellective appetite, called the will, as stated in I:82:5, those movements which occur in the lower appetite, are with passion, while those in the higher appetite are without passion, as shown above (I:87:2 ad 1; I-II:22:3 ad 3). Now the act of the virtue of hope cannot belong to the sensitive appetite, since the good which is the principal object of this virtue, is not a sensible but a Divine good. Therefore hope resides in the higher appetite called the will, and not in the lower appetite, of which the irascible is a part.[^newadvent]
