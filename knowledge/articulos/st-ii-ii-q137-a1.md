# Respondeo (excerpt)

I answer that, According to the Philosopher (Ethic. ii, 3), " virtue is about the difficult and the good "; and so where there is a special kind of difficulty or goodness, there is a special virtue. Now a virtuous deed may involve goodness or difficulty on two counts. First, from the act's very species, which is considered in respect of the proper object of that act: secondly, from the length of time, since to persist long in something difficult involves a special difficulty. Hence to persist long in something good until it is accomplished belongs to a special virtue. […][^newadvent]
