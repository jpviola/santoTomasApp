# Respondeo (excerpt)

I answer that, To say that the soul is of the Divine substance involves a manifest improbability. For, as is clear from what has been said (I:77:2; I:79:2; I:84:6), the human soul is sometimes in a state of potentiality to the act of intelligence —acquires its knowledge somehow from things—and thus has various powers; all of which are incompatible with the Divine Nature, Which is a pure act —receives nothing from any other—and admits of no variety in itself, as we have proved (I:3:1 and I:3:7; I:9:1). This error seems to have originated from two statements of the ancients. For those who first began to observe the nature of things, being unable to rise above their imagination, supposed that nothing but bodies existed. Therefore they said that God was a body, which they considered to be the principle of other bodies. […][^newadvent]
