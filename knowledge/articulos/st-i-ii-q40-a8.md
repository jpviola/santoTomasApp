# Respondeo (excerpt)

I answer that, Hope of its very nature is a help to action by making it more intense: and this for two reasons. First, by reason of its object, which is a good, difficult but possible. For the thought of its being difficult arouses our attention; while the thought that it is possible is no drag on our effort. Hence it follows that by reason of hope man is intent on his action. Secondly, on account of its effect. Because hope, as stated above (I-II:32:3), causes pleasure; which is a help to action, as stated above (I-II:33:4). Therefore hope is conducive to action.[^newadvent]
